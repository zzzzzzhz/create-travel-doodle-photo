# Travel Doodle Photo Style Specification

## Fixed identity

Create the look of a real documentary or editorial photograph that has been physically annotated and collaged by a young travel-magazine art director. Preserve natural light, believable materials, skin texture, spatial depth, lens perspective, and everyday life. Never convert the whole image into an illustration.

## Approved intensity

- Keep the photographic base visually dominant at roughly 64–69%.
- Keep the total graphic layer at roughly 31–36%.
- Use an energetic but controlled arrangement: lively, slightly messy, and visibly handmade, yet intentionally art-directed.
- Maintain one clear focal point and a readable foreground, middle ground, and background.

## Color system

- Bright imperfect yellow: primary contour, emphasis, direction, star, and burst color.
- Cobalt blue and teal/cyan: broad brush energy, environmental flow, sound wave, water, road, wind, or depth color.
- Orange: motion ticks, heat, sparks, impact, hand movement, wheel rhythm, and warm accents.
- Rough black ink: arrows, dotted paths, quotation marks, music notes, flame extensions, clouds, and abstract symbols.
- Magenta/deep pink: sparse sticker or tape accent only. Never let it become a large color block.

## Mark-making

- Make yellow contours thick, selective, slightly broken, and imperfect. Avoid a clean vector glow around every pixel of the subject.
- Use broad blue/teal brush strokes mainly behind or beside the focal subject.
- Use orange marks as short reactions to real action rather than generic decoration.
- Draw black arrows and dotted routes with rough ink texture. Make them point toward an actual destination, reflection, motion, gaze, or object relationship.
- Use stars, notes, quotation marks, clouds, lightning, and abstract symbols sparingly and contextually.
- Preserve tactile marker bleed, dry-brush gaps, paper fibers, ink variation, rough cut edges, and handmade irregularity.

## Scene-specific flow

Derive the graphic flow from the photograph:

- People walking or posing: trace the collective silhouette and connect steps, arms, gaze, and crosswalk rhythm.
- Vehicles: follow wheel rotation, body direction, road curve, wind, and travel path.
- Mirrors or windows: orbit the frame and direct arrows toward the reflection or view.
- Speakers or music: radiate rings and wave shapes from the sound source.
- Cooking: extend flame, wok arc, ingredient flight, and hand movement.
- Signs or architecture: follow frame edges, wiring, perspective, and upward/downward direction.
- Animals: preserve anatomy and use gait, tail, paws, ears, or gaze as the motion source.
- Quiet portraits: connect light, steam, reading direction, plants, or gaze with thinner calmer marks.
- Nature or underwater scenes: follow wind, mist, water current, bubbles, waves, or terrain contours.

## Torn-paper rule

- Use zero, one, or two beige torn-paper accents; never exceed two.
- Make each piece only 2.5–4% of the canvas height.
- Keep the combined paper area below 6% of the image.
- Place paper only near safe outer corners or edges.
- Put at most one tiny black doodle or music note on each piece.
- Use only a tiny magenta tape strip.
- Never place paper over a face, hand, animal, vehicle, product, architecture focal point, or existing text.

## Hard exclusions

Avoid all of the following:

- oversized torn paper, giant paper numerals, giant letters, headline typography, or a dominant scrapbook card;
- a graphic layer so heavy that the original photograph loses its atmosphere;
- a graphic layer so light that the result looks like a few random stickers;
- repeating the same arrow/star arrangement regardless of scene;
- clean corporate vector lines, perfect computer gradients, flat poster blocks, or generic social-media templates;
- covering faces, eyes, hands, fingers, existing text, license plates, products, food, animals, or vehicle details;
- changing identities, expressions, anatomy, clothing, vehicle structure, architecture, crop, or perspective during an edit;
- duplicated people or objects, extra limbs, malformed hands, inaccurate wheels, fake reflections, unreadable replaced text, borders, or white margins.
